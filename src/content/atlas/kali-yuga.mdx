## An age within a cycle
What changes when an age is understood as one part of a much larger story?

In the common Puranic model, Kali follows Satya, Treta, and Dvapara. All four together form a Maha Yuga. Kali is therefore a particular age within a repeating structure, not another name for the entire universe's lifetime.

## How long is Kali Yuga?
Vishnu Purana I.3 describes its main period and two transitions as 1,200 divine years in total. Using the chapter's conversion of 360 human years to a divine year gives **432,000 human years**. This is a traditional cosmological measure, not a scientific estimate of Earth's age.

## Why is it associated with difficulty?
Vishnu Purana VI.1 describes an age in which wealth, power, and appearances can displace integrity and religious practice. Its account belongs to a particular text and tradition. It should not be used as a shortcut for labeling every modern event or judging the worth of individual people.

## Does difficulty remove hope?
The following chapter changes the emphasis. Vishnu Purana VI.2 praises the spiritual value of devotion to Vishnu in the Kali age. Read together, the chapters offer both a warning about conduct and a devotional response to difficult conditions.

This is a Vaishnava framing. Other traditions may emphasize different practices and interpretations. The page's purpose is to make that distinction visible, rather than prescribe one path for every reader.

## Why context matters
Kali Yuga belongs inside a Maha Yuga; Maha Yugas belong within larger time cycles. An ending at one scale is not automatically the end of everything. Return to the Four Yugas map whenever the scale becomes confusing.

## A question for the present
As an original reflection, this framework can invite a practical question: what does honest, considerate action look like when circumstances are difficult? That reflection is an invitation to thoughtful conduct, not a prediction or a translated passage.
