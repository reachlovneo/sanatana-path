## Start with one idea

Cosmic time is **cycles within cycles**.

The four Yugas form one Maha Yuga. Maha Yugas repeat within a Manvantara.
Fourteen Manvantaras unfold within one Kalpa, commonly described as one day of
Brahma.

You do not need to memorize enormous numbers first. Understand the structure
first.

## The deeper architecture

The Vishnu Purana's time-measure account presents the four Yugas as one great
age, or Maha Yuga. A thousand such cycles form a day of Brahma. Within that day
are fourteen Manvantaras. At the close of the day comes a period of dissolution
and a night of equal duration, after which creation begins again.

The important first insight is not the arithmetic. It is the structure:

1. A smaller cycle can belong inside a larger cycle.
2. A period of activity can be followed by rest or dissolution.
3. An ending can be part of a rhythm rather than the absolute end of meaning.

## Two useful ways to see time

Linear time helps us ask: **What happened before, and what happens next?**

Cyclical time helps us ask: **What pattern are we inside?**

Both questions can be useful. A life still has an unfolding story, choices still
have consequences, and moments do not simply repeat in an identical way.
Cyclical time adds another scale of understanding: change itself belongs to a
larger order.

## Why describe such immense scales?

Vast cosmic measures can create distance from the urgency of ordinary life.
That distance can be misunderstood as insignificance: *If time is so vast, does
my life matter?*

The more useful question may be the opposite: *Within a world of constant
change, what quality should I bring to the moment I actually have?*

Cosmic scale can hold two truths together:

- **Humility:** one lifetime is not the center of all existence.
- **Responsibility:** this lifetime is still where intention, action, care, and
  understanding become real for us.

## Three ways to approach the model

### As cosmology

The cycles can be studied as a traditional account of vast cosmic periods and
their relationships.

### As sacred imagination

The scale can expand the imagination beyond immediate concerns and place human
life within a sacred order.

### As a reflective lens

The pattern of arising, continuity, dissolution, and renewal can help us notice
similar rhythms in seasons, communities, projects, identities, and inner life.
This reflective use should not be confused with claiming that every personal
event directly corresponds to a cosmic cycle.

You do not need to hold the complete chronology in your mind yet. Keep only
